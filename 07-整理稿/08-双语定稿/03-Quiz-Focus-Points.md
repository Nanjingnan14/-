## 1. Food Security and Food Self-Sufficiency (W10)

### Quote and Source

"This will probably definitely be in your quiz, so make sure you can tell the difference." (W10 06:38–06:44) Before this sentence she first said "It's important that we get these two concepts right because they are two different concepts" (06:15–06:19), and only then went on to the definitions.

### Minimum Content to Master

| Item | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Definition of food security | "Food security exists when all people, at all times, have physical and economic access to sufficient, safe and nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and food preferences for an active and healthy life" (World Food Summit, 1996) | L10 slide 6 |
| Three qualifiers | all people (not just the aggregate), at all times (not just the current year), physical and economic access (not just output) | L10 slide 6 |
| Non-food inputs | The teacher adds that in judging whether a community is food secure, "sometimes we also include non-food inputs" | W10 08:09 |
| Definition of food self-sufficiency | "the extent to which a country can satisfy its food needs from its own domestic production" (FAO 1999; Clapp 2017) | L10 slide 8 |
| The 100% line | production = consumption | L10 slide 8 |
| Counter-example | Singapore ranked highest on the food security index in 2019, yet 90% of its food is imported (CAN 2019); Hong Kong likewise | L10 slide 10; W10 15:00–15:02 |
| Consequence | Countries highly dependent on imports are more exposed to food price volatility | L10 slide 10 |

### The Teacher's Own Explanatory Sentences

"because a community can have high food security but no self-sufficiency, right? So Singapore is a very good example." (W10 14:23–14:37)
"In fact, in 2019, it has the highest food security in the world, but actually, 90% of its food is imported" (W10 14:43–14:48)

### Possible MC Formats

1. Give the sentence 'A country's grain output reaches 120% of consumption, therefore its level of food security is high' and ask where it goes wrong. The answer points to self-sufficiency and security not being the same thing.
2. Give the 2019 figures for Singapore and ask what they best demonstrate. The answer points to the two being able to diverge.
3. Give the 1996 definition with "economic access" removed and replaced by "sufficient production", and ask which criterion is lost after the change.

### Essay Question and Skeleton

Question: "Food security and food self-sufficiency are not the same thing. Discuss with reference to the corporate food regime."
Skeleton: first distinguish the definitions (the three qualifiers against production=consumption), then use Singapore to show the divergence, then move to who decides what is grown and who sets the standards under the corporate food regime, and finally point out that the self-sufficiency rate remains the most convenient indicator for political mobilisation.

### Revision Action

Turn to L10 slides 6, 8 and 10 and read each of the two definitions once; then look at slide 18 on the three waves of supermarkets and slide 16 on the corporate food regime, and explain why "security" depends increasingly on import capacity rather than on the self-sufficiency rate.

## 2. Import-Substitution Industrialization and Export-Oriented Industrialization (W3 + W4, Two Points)

### Quote and Source

"why are we talking about these industrialization models that are 40, 50, 60 years ago? Why is it relevant? Well, number one, it's relevant because it's in your quiz." (W3 26:29–26:39)
"When you do your quiz, you're sort of not sure which model is which, then this is a helpful table for you." (W4 08:14) This sentence refers to the ISI/EOI comparison table.

### Minimum Content to Master

| Item | ISI | EOI |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Replace imports with domestic production, reduce dependence on the first world | Produce manufactured goods and export them, aiming at a larger international market |
| Three instruments | Tariffs, quotas, content regulation (requiring imported goods to contain local content) | Also protects domestic industry, but sets a time limit |
| Form of support | Licensing that limits the number of firms in a sector, subsidies, cheap loans to support "infant industries" | The state "picks winners", concentrating support on a few strategic sectors |
| State conditions | Even a weak state can announce it, but cannot enforce it | Requires a strong/hard state able to discipline private capital (Chibber) |
| Typical cases | 1930s Turkey and Latin America, 1940–1970 Chile, post-independence Africa, India's First Five-Year Plan | 1960s Mexico and Brazil turn, South Korea, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore |
| Consequences | Absence of competition leads to inefficiency, captive market, rent-seeking corruption, inequality | Labour-intensive production brings a large middle-income group and forms an internal engine of growth |
| And distribution | Capital-intensive, wealth concentrated, GDP looks good while most people do not benefit | Labour-intensive, wages flow to workers and consumers |

Source: L3 slides 22–32, 36–45; W3 26:29–26:39; W4 08:14–09:27.

### The Teacher's Own Explanatory Sentences

"Substitution is about producing their own goods instead of importing from the first world. They use instruments like tariffs and quotas to restrict foreign imports… and local governments give a lot of support to local industries, by licensing schemes, by subsidies, and by providing a lot of cheap loans" (W4 08:22–08:48; the transcript records tariffs as terrors, quotas as quarters, and infant industries as local babies)
"Export-oriented industrialization also believe in producing their own goods but they put a strong focus on manufactured goods and… on exporting these goods rather than just selling it to the local market." (W4 08:54–09:08)
"Just like ISI, they also protect local industries but the government is very clear that we're going to only protect you for a short period of time." (W4 09:17–09:27)

### Possible MC Formats

1. List four instruments (tariffs, quotas, content regulation, export tax rebates) and ask which one is not among the three instruments of ISI.
2. Give 'the government protects domestic industry but sets no time limit' and ask whether this looks more like ISI or EOI, or which condition of EOI it lacks.
3. Give 'labour-intensive export orientation makes wages flow to the majority' and ask which model this distributional outcome corresponds to.
4. Write 'a strong state disciplines capital' as 'the state withdraws from the market' and ask where this goes wrong.

### Essay Question and Skeleton

Question: "Compare ISI and EOI as development strategies. Why did some East Asian economies move upward in the world system while much of Latin America and Africa did not?"
Skeleton: first set out the two sets of instruments and conditions side by side, then introduce state capacity as the intervening variable (Chibber's strong state and time-limited protection), use South Korea against Latin America and Africa, and finally point out that East Asia's ascent depended at once on Cold War geopolitical conditions and on a labour-intensive distributional structure.

### Revision Action

Lay L3 slides 22–32 next to the comparison table at W4 08:14 and read them together, then write out from memory the three instruments and the four conditions for the turn; then turn to L3 slide 34 (per capita GDP of four places) and slide 38 (Hong Kong manufacturing data) as examples.

## 3. Foucault and Postmodernism (W5)

### Quote and Source

"you need to read him now for this class because it will be in your quiz, so even if you don't want to read him, you have to" (W5 L360, referring to Foucault)

### Minimum Content to Master

| Item | Content | Source |
|---|---|---|
| The postmodern claim | "There is no objective reality"; social reality is constructed through language; knowledge is always situated and particular, not absolutely universal | L5 slide 18 |
| The three sentences on discourse | "A group of statements which provide a language for talking about a particular topic at a particular historical moment"; discourse "constructs the topic" and "governs the way that a topic can be meaningfully talked about and reasoned about"; "Discourse is about the production of knowledge through language" | L5 slide 20 |
| Relation to power | power-knowledge nexus, "people and institutions have the power to define the terms of the debate or the way a problem is to be understood" (Watson 2000: 70) | L5 slide 20 |
| Development as discourse | PD theory treats development as a discourse; statements about development and the Third World are largely a Eurocentric construction | L5 slide 21 |
| Two representatives | Escobar (Colombia/Latin America), Ferguson (Lesotho/Africa) | L5 slide 21 |
| Example of a word chain | Protest / Demonstration / Riot / Threat to public security / Civil disobedience / Social movement, six words naming the same event with different characters | L5 slide 17 |

### The Teacher's Own Explanatory Sentences

"post-modernism argues… it's the concept that you can uncover objective scientific truth about the world… post-modernism challenges that, actually there is no objective reality, because there is no reality outside of language" (W5 lines 365–375)
"you need to read him now for this class because it will be in your quiz" (W5 L360)

### Possible MC Formats

1. Give 'discourse constructs its object and limits what can be discussed' and ask whose formulation this is or which theory it belongs to.
2. Give 'change one word and the character of the same event changes' and ask which mechanism of discourse this expresses.
3. Write 'postmodernism advocates a return to premodern community' as the correct option and ask where this goes wrong (postmodernism does not advocate a return to the premodern; it holds that there is no objective reality outside language).

### Essay Question and Skeleton

Question: "Ferguson argues that development projects depoliticise poverty. Discuss with reference to the anti-politics machine and post-development's view of discourse."
Skeleton: first give the two mechanisms of discourse (constructing the object, limiting what can be said), then use Lesotho to show how technicisation turns a political problem into a technical one, then give post-development's account of this machine, and finally use one of the four criticisms to point out post-development's own blind spot (romanticising premodern community).

### Revision Action

Turn to L5 slides 17–21 and memorise the three definitional sentences on discourse well enough to write them out; then set the Lesotho case on slides 31–36 in two columns, 'what discourse says / what reality is'.
