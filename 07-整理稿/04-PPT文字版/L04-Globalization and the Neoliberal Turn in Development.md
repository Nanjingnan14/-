## 第 19 张｜EMBEDDED LIBERALISM

EMBEDDED LIBERALISM
▪The greatest fear amongst European powers after WW2 (1939-1945) was the recurrence of the Great Depression
▪The “self-regulating” market had produced disasters like the Great
Depression → Society need to protect itself from unregulated market activities
▪A more interventionist approach to counter socially unacceptable aspects of the market
“Embed”: To fix firmly in something, to enclose
“Embedded liberalism”: “market processes and entrepreneurial and corporate activities were surrounded by a web of social and political constraints and a regulatory environment” (Harvey 2005: 11)

## 第 21 张｜▪Keynes had no problem with the market economy, but:

▪Keynes had no problem with the market economy, but:

## 第 27 张｜NEOLIBERALISM

NEOLIBERALISM
In this context, rise of neoliberalism as a new ideology:
▪Low taxes and tax cuts, especially for the wealthy
→ encourage people to earn more → invest and spend more
▪Tax cuts for business and industry
▪Spending on welfare should be minimized
→ without safety nets the poor would be forced to find work
▪Privatization of public sectors such as transport, telecommunications, oil, social housing, education, etc. → open up these areas for business and profit-making. Create markets if they do not exist.
▪Opposed to groups that operate to restrain business interests and their efforts to accumulate capital (e.g. labor unions)

## 第 29 张｜STATE & MARKET UNDER NEOLIBERALISM

STATE & MARKET UNDER NEOLIBERALISM
▪Free market: A market free of any impediments will improve economic welfare, bring greater individual freedom
▪Deregulation: Commitment by nation-states to limit or eliminate restraints on the free market and free trade
▪Limited government: No government can do things as well as the market and should not intervene in it
▪“The fundamental mission of the neoliberal state is to create a ‘good business climate’ and therefore to optimize conditions for capital accumulation” (Harvey 2006: 25)
▪Contrasts with the social democratic state: emphasizes full employment and welfare provision
▪Market fundamentalism: “Markets will take care of all our needs”

## 第 42 张｜WHY DIDN’T IT WORK?

WHY DIDN’T IT WORK?
▪Privatization often turns state enterprises from losses to profits by trimming the payroll
▪Huge social costs associated with unemployment, which private firms do not have to take into account
▪With minimal job protections, employers can dismiss workers, with little or no costs, with minimal severance pay
▪Privatization of monopolies may be more efficient in performing their functions than governments, but they are also more efficient in exploiting their monopoly positions
▪Profit or “shareholder value” maximization
▪Consumers suffer as a result

## 第 53 张｜NEOLIBERALISM AND DEVELOPMENT

NEOLIBERALISM AND DEVELOPMENT
▪The development state after WW2:
▪Growth strategy favoring industrialization
▪State played major role in managing the economy and mobilizing resources to support industrial growth
▪SAP “reversed” the development project as it prescribed “shrinking the state”
▪Power within the state was redistributed: privileging the central bank, trade and finance ministers > program-oriented ministries (social services, agriculture, education)
▪Weakens the political coalitions of the national development state
▪Removes resources from state agencies that support social sectors, with harmful effects on the majority of citizens
McMichael (2012: 56)

## 第 54 张｜▪Deregulated markets in restructured economies of the global

▪Deregulated markets in restructured economies of the global South became profitable for foreign investments:
▪By 1992, more than 80 countries had privatized almost 7,000 public enterprises, mostly public services e.g. water, electricity, telephone.
▪Rapid privatization extended the reach of foreign ownership of assets in developing countries, exactly what the latter were trying to overcome in the
1970s
▪SAPs at the same time opened up developing countries to Northern-imposed disciplines:
▪SAPs as a method of governing: Rise of “global managers”, e.g. IMF and
World Bank officials, G7 political elites, global bankers, executives of TNCs
▪Citizens in these countries did not elect IMF or or the World Bank, and yet these institutions dictate the policies
McMichael (2012: 127-9)
