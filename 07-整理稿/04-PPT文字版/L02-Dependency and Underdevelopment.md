## 第 6 张｜CRITIQUING MODERNIZATION APPROACH

CRITIQUING MODERNIZATION APPROACH
Focus on internal sources of change within the nation-state: Development seen as a domestic matter.
Krishna (2009): Rostow basically treats different nations as runners positioned in distinct lanes in a race track
▪Each country runs in its own lane
▪Some countries start the race earlier (Britain →
Germany and US …) while others are just entering the race or heading to the stadium
▪The track condition is the same for all
→ Countries have themselves to blame for their success or failure in taking off
[图]

## 第 7 张｜Methodological nationalism:

Methodological nationalism:
“The essential point is that each country’s development is seen as a discrete and largely self-contained project. Each country seems to step on to the race track in conditions deemed identical, whether it was in the late 18th century or the mid-20th century: time literally stands still for it. Rostow does not entertain the notion that earlier development of some countries may have irrevocably altered the prospects of later developers. It is in that sense a static and ahistorical model.” (Krishna 2009: 14).

## 第 26 张｜Frank’s answer: It is NOT a coincidence

Frank’s answer: It is NOT a coincidence
▪Development (of the west) and underdevelopment (of the rest) are two sides of the same process, resulting in polarization
▪Many “traditional” countries were quite advanced before they encountered colonialism in the 18th century, e.g. India and China
▪The reason for their underdevelopment is external: Colonialism and foreign domination have reversed development in Third World countries, and forced them on a path of economic backwardness
▪Underdevelopment is not natural: it is the result of a long history of colonial domination

## 第 33 张｜Underdeveloping industries in colonies:

Underdeveloping industries in colonies:
Forced colonies into primary commodity production
Example: Handicraft decline in India and the rise of the British East India Company from 1750s onwards

## 第 34 张｜“This period, coinciding with the first Industrial Revolutio

“This period, coinciding with the first Industrial Revolution in Britain, marked the decisive colonization and underdevelopment of India. From here on, the Indian economy was steadily extraverted – that is, designed to meet the economic needs of Britain rather than her own domestic population.
India was forced to specialize in the export of minerals, raw materials, and agricultural commodities, often at unremunerative prices, and to import industrial or manufactured goods from Britain.
“In a matter of a few decades… India’s once flourishing textile and handcrafts industries were wiped out by the cheap machine-made imports from Britain. Almost overnight, India went from being one of the world’s largest exporters of textiles and clothing to being a major exporter of raw cotton and an importer of finished goods.” (Krishna 2009:18)

## 第 36 张｜THE METROPOLIS-SATELLITE MODEL

THE METROPOLIS-SATELLITE MODEL
▪Frank’s model of how underdevelopment works
▪Originated in the colonial era, when colonial powers implanted new cities in Third World countries to facilitate the transfer of economic surplus to the West
▪These national cities serve as satellites to the
Western metropolis
▪National cities are in turn served by provincial cities, and provincial cities by local cities
▪A whole chain of constellations established to extract raw materials, commodities, profits from
Third World villages to local capitals, to regional capitals, to national capitals, and finally to the cities of Western countries

## 第 39 张｜INTELLECTUAL HERITAGE

INTELLECTUAL HERITAGE
Classical Marxism and Neo-Marxism
Karl Marx on capitalist forms of production:
▪Society divided into two classes: those who own the means of production
(bourgeoisie) and those who do not (proletariat)
▪The proletariat class must sell their labor, i.e. work to earn a wage. They work not only to provide for themselves and their family, but also to provide for the bourgeoisie
▪This “surplus” – the amount of work beyond that needed to meet basic needs – creates profits for the owners, because workers are not paid the full value of the goods they produced
Neo-Marxists:
▪Argue that capitalism was in a period of “monopoly capitalism” where large companies dominated and exploited poorer countries (Baran and Sweezy 1968)
▪Only solution is to break away from the world capitalist system in favor of a state-socialist system → revolution

## 第 45 张｜HISTORICAL CONTEXT

HISTORICAL CONTEXT
▪1950s: Modernization school dominated the field of development
▪Under the Cold War context and the rise of US as world power
▪Modernization theory justifies the asymmetrical power relations between “traditional” and “modern” societies: Because we are advanced and modern, and you are traditional and backward, you should look to us for guidance.
▪Helps legitimate the expansion of US power through foreign aid and intervention: need of capital and exposure to modern values to achieve “take-off”
▪1960s: The failure of modernization programs in Latin America gave birth to the neo-Marxist dependency school

## 第 49 张｜Each region plays a functionally specific role in the worldw

Each region plays a functionally specific role in the worldwide division of labour:
▪Core: Most economically advanced, focus on high-skill, capital-intensive production
(information, finance, service industries).
Significant means of influence over periphery.
▪Periphery: Least economically developed, focus on low-skill, labor-intensive production and extraction of raw materials. Extensively influenced by core countries and their multinational corporations.
▪Semi-periphery: Midway between core and periphery. More economically developed and less dependent on core countries than periphery.
[图]

## 第 50 张｜▪Unbalanced economic structure between core and periphery

▪Unbalanced economic structure between core and periphery
▪Core countries exploit periphery countries in terms of human and natural resources
▪Periphery countries are financially and technologically dependent on core countries
▪This imbalance limits development in periphery countries, resulting in “underdevelopment” and “dependent development”
▪The semi-periphery acts as a buffer between core and periphery
▪Exploited by core, but also exploits periphery
▪Partially deflects political pressure and prevents periphery countries from forming a united front against the core
▪Helps stabilize the global capitalist system by acting as a political buffer zone

## 第 51 张｜▪The three-tiered model allows for potential movement from o

▪The three-tiered model allows for potential movement from one category to another:
▪Position of a state is not fixed, countries do not follow a linear pathway of progress
▪Upward and downward mobility: When the global economy changes, some countries may make economic advances, while others may lose out
▪Avoids the deterministic statement of dependency theory, i.e.
periphery is bound to suffer underdevelopment because core countries always exploit it
▪Can study why some countries (e.g. East Asia) are able to rise above their peripheral status
