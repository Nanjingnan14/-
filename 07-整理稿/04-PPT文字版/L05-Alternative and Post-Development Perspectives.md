## 第 3 张｜STATE & MARKET UNDER NEOLIBERALISM

STATE & MARKET UNDER NEOLIBERALISM
▪Free market: A market free of any impediments will improve economic welfare, bring greater individual freedom
▪Deregulation: Commitment by nation-states to limit or eliminate restraints on the free market and free trade
▪Limited government: No government can do things as well as the market and should not intervene in it
▪“The fundamental mission of the neoliberal state is to create a ‘good business climate’ and therefore to optimize conditions for capital accumulation” (Harvey 2006:
25)
▪Contrasts with the social democratic state: emphasizes full employment and welfare provision
▪Market fundamentalism: “Markets will take care of all our needs”

## 第 4 张｜EXPORT TO REST OF WORLD

EXPORT TO REST OF WORLD
▪Third World countries were lent a large amount of money through
IMF and World Bank in the 1960s and 1970s
▪Loans were used to fund public development projects: subways, ports, telecommunication networks, etc. Also used to strengthen the military.
▪Debt crisis in the 1980s:
▪Fall in world commodity prices in the late 1970s: loss of earnings for developing countries
▪Global recession of 1980-81: developed countries adopted protectionist measures;
made it harder for developing countries to export their goods
→Developing countries were not able to meet the repayments on their debt

## 第 5 张｜STRUCTURAL ADJUSTMENT PROGRAMS (SAPS)

STRUCTURAL ADJUSTMENT PROGRAMS (SAPS)
▪The debt crisis provided the context for the widespread application of neoliberalism in developing countries
▪IMF and World Bank as major actors implementing SAPs in the mid-
1980s
SAPs: “a comprehensive restructuring of production priorities and government programs in a debtor country”
▪The philosophy underlying SAPs = the market ideologies adopted by the Thatcher and Reagan administrations

## 第 6 张｜NEOLIBERALISM AND DEVELOPMENT

NEOLIBERALISM AND DEVELOPMENT
▪The development state after WW2:
▪Growth strategy favoring industrialization
▪State played major role in managing the economy and mobilizing resources to support industrial growth
▪SAP “reversed” the development project as it prescribed “shrinking the state”
▪Power within the state was redistributed: privileging the central bank, trade and finance ministers > program-oriented ministries (social services, agriculture, education)
▪Weakens the political coalitions of the national development state
▪Removes resources from state agencies that support social sectors, with harmful effects on the majority of citizens
McMichael (2012: 56)

## 第 7 张｜▪Deregulated markets in restructured economies of the global

▪Deregulated markets in restructured economies of the global South became profitable for foreign investments:
▪By 1992, more than 80 countries had privatized almost 7,000 public enterprises, mostly public services e.g. water, electricity, telephone.
▪Rapid privatization extended the reach of foreign ownership of assets in developing countries, exactly what the latter were trying to overcome in the 1970s
▪SAPs at the same time opened up developing countries to Northern-imposed disciplines:
▪SAPs as a method of governing: Rise of “global managers”, e.g. IMF and World Bank officials, G7 political elites, global bankers, executives of TNCs
▪Citizens in these countries did not elect IMF or or the World Bank, and yet these institutions dictate the policies
McMichael (2012: 127-9)

## 第 13 张｜▪Post-development thought emerged in the 1990s

▪Post-development thought emerged in the 1990s
▪Parallel to the rise of anti-globalization movements
▪Gained momentum after the Asian financial crisis
▪Calls for reassertion of local autonomy in the face of what is seen as the homogenizing tendencies of globalization
▪The two shared similarities:
▪Challenge the whole development project
▪See it as destructive to traditional societies and natural environments
▪Emphasize the local, the particular, the grassroots

## 第 20 张｜Discourse (論述/話語)

Discourse (論述/話語)
▪“A group of statements which provide a language for talking about a particular topic at a particular historical moment”
▪Discourse “constructs the topic” and “governs the way that a topic can be meaningfully talked about and reasoned about” (Livia and Hall
1997)
▪“Discourse is about the production of knowledge through language”
•Directly linked to power: power-knowledge nexus where “people and institutions have the power to define the terms of the debate or the way a problem is to be understood.” (Watson 2000: 70)

## 第 24 张｜1. FORMS OF KNOWLEDGE

1. FORMS OF KNOWLEDGE
▪The notions of “underdevelopment” and “Third World” were discursive products of the post-WW2 period (p. 31)
▪Before 1945, these concepts did not exist
▪The First/Second/Third World division was made by a French demographer in 1952
▪Third World became equated with poor, non-industrialized nations (vs free industrialized nations and Communist industrialized nations)
▪Problematization of poverty:
▪The essential trait of Third World = poverty
▪Solution = economic growth and development

## 第 27 张｜2. SYSTEM OF POWER

2. SYSTEM OF POWER
▪The system “sets the rules that must be followed for this or that problem, theory, or object to emerge and be named, analyzed, and eventually transformed into a policy or a plan” (p. 41)
▪Various actors:
▪Experts, universities, research centers
▪International organizations: e.g. United Nations, which had the “moral, professional, and legal authority to name subjects and define strategies”
▪Lending organizations: control capital and power
▪First World governments: with power to decide what was to be done
▪Third World governments: had legal political authority over their subjects

## 第 35 张｜Very different from the reality academic scholars agreed wit

Very different from the reality academic scholars agreed with:

## 第 50 张｜CRITIQUES OF POST-DEVELOPMENT

CRITIQUES OF POST-DEVELOPMENT
▪Complete rejection of modernity and development
▪Ignores many positive aspects development brought about: e.g. advances in technology, medicine; lower infant mortality; education…
▪Nederveen Pieterse (1998): Escobar refers to development as
“Development”, but in reality, there are many approaches to development, different applications at different times in different societies around the world
▪Uncritical stance towards local communities and cultural tradition
▪While demonizing industrial modernity, PD romanticizes premodern communities
▪Rejecting universalism and celebrating cultural difference →danger of concealing or accepting the oppression legitimized by tradition/culture
▪“Postmodern-influenced critiques of modern science and modernization are laying the foundations of a neo-populist movement that seeks to subordinate modernization to the anti-modernist and patriarchal values of the traditional elites” (Nanda, cited in Ziai 2004: 1056)
