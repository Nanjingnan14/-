## 第 24 张｜MEASUREMENTS OF DEVELOPMENT

MEASUREMENTS OF DEVELOPMENT
▪Gross Domestic Product (GDP)
Value of all goods and services produced within a country, regardless of whether the profits are claimed by national or foreign individuals/firms
▪Gross National Product (GNP) or Gross National Income (GNI)
Value of all goods and services claimed by residents of a country, regardless of where production took place (includes income overseas)
▪Development viewed largely in economic terms
▪The World Bank uses GNI per capita to categorize countries: low-income/ lower-middle-income/ upper-middle-income/ high-income.
▪Assumption that greater economic wealth equates better livelihoods, health, education, etc.
Willis (2005: 3-4)

## 第 35 张｜THE DEVELOPMENT PROJECT AFTER WW2

THE DEVELOPMENT PROJECT AFTER WW2
▪Decolonization:
Former colonies gaining independence as sovereign “nation states”
1945-1981: 105 new states joined the United Nations
Age of optimism: Governments and peoples coming together to stimulate economic growth; improve education, healthcare, housing, transportation;
promote democracy and citizenship in the newly formed nation states.
“Development” as an emancipatory promise: based on “the ideal of self-governing states composed of citizens united by the ideology of nationalism” vs. colonial rule over subjects (“civilizing inferior races”)
McMichael (2005: 4, 13)

## 第 36 张｜▪Establishment of the United Nations

▪Establishment of the United Nations
UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)
“A new world paradigm of fundamental rights of freedom, equality, life, liberty, and security to all, without distinction by race, color, sex, language, religion, political opinion, national or social origin, property, birth, or other status” (McMichael 2012: 42)
Right to a social contract: entitlement to economic, social and cultural rights
Birth of the GNP (Gross National Product)
A universal, quantitative measurement of “development” across national contexts
Binding diverse countries together by a standard, measured by national economic growth and income levels (governed by the market and its metrics)

## 第 44 张｜HISTORICAL CONTEXT

HISTORICAL CONTEXT
▪Modernization theory reached its heyday in the decades after the end of WW2
▪Historical product of three developments in the post-WW2 era:
▪The rise of the United States as the world leader
▪The spread of a worldwide communist movement, led by the Soviet Union

▪The US government and private foundations poured funding to support a new generation of young economists, political scientists, sociologists, etc. to study Third World states, to promote economic development and political stability

## 第 47 张｜1. Traditional society

1. Traditional society
▪Agricultural society
▪Pre-Newtonian science and technology
▪Family and clan relationships; “pre-nation state”
2. Preconditions for take-off
▪National level organizations and institutions
▪Rise of individualistic values: rational, risk taking, able to defer pleasure and immediate consumption
▪Savings and investment rates above population growth rates

## 第 48 张｜3. Take-off

3. Take-off
▪Stimulus to take-off needed:
➢Political revolution and restructuring of major institutions
➢Technological innovation
➢Favorable international environment with rising export demands and prices
▪Critical factor: rate of productive investments and savings must reach 10% of national income (otherwise economic growth cannot overtake rate of population growth)

## 第 49 张｜4. Drive to maturity

4. Drive to maturity
▪Once growth has become an automatic process, the fourth stage is reached (“cruising altitude”)
▪Extended range of technology adopted; development of new sectors
5. Age of high mass consumption
▪Growth in employment opportunities
▪Increase in national income
▪Formation of strong domestic market; widespread consumption of consumer goods and services
▪Increase in welfare spending

## 第 55 张｜CRITIQUING MODERNIZATION APPROACH

CRITIQUING MODERNIZATION APPROACH
▪Modernization is essentially understood as Westernization
▪Highly general, does not pay attention to the range of societies in the
South, their histories, cultures, and needs
▪James Scott’s critique of “high modernism”:
▪Overconfidence in science and technical planning in redesigning society
▪Central plans ignore local complexities
▪Technical knowledge (techne) vs Practical local knowledge (mētis): The first being formal, universal, scientific; the latter experiential and context-specific

## 第 56 张｜CRITIQUING MODERNIZATION APPROACH

CRITIQUING MODERNIZATION APPROACH
▪Focus on internal sources of change within the nation-state: Development seen as a domestic matter.
▪Krishna (2009): Rostow basically treats different nations as runners positioned in distinct lanes in a race track
▪Each country runs in its own lane
▪Some countries start the race earlier (Britain →
Germany and US …) while others are just entering the race or heading to the stadium
▪The track condition is the same for all
→ Countries have themselves to blame for their success or failure in taking off
[图]

## 第 57 张｜Methodological nationalism:

Methodological nationalism:
“The essential point is that each country’s development is seen as a discrete and largely self-contained project. Each country seems to step on to the race track in conditions deemed identical, whether it was in the late 18th century or the mid-20th century: time literally stands still for it. Rostow does not entertain the notion that earlier development of some countries may have irrevocably altered the prospects of later developers. It is in that sense a static and ahistorical model.” (Krishna 2009: 14).
