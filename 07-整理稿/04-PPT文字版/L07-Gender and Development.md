## 第 11 张｜APPROACHES TO GENDER AND DEVELOPMENT

APPROACHES TO GENDER AND DEVELOPMENT
▪The influence of feminism on development began with the first UN
World Conference on Women, held in Mexico City in 1975
▪1950s and 1960s:
▪Welfare measures mainly targeted women in their domestic role
▪Addressing practical gender needs, e.g. food aid, health, nutrition
▪Women largely viewed as passive
▪1970s:
▪UN declared 1976-1985 the “UN Decade for Women”
▪Women in Development (WID) movement
McMichael (2012)

## 第 12 张｜WOMEN IN DEVELOPMENT (WID)

WOMEN IN DEVELOPMENT (WID)
▪Agenda of equality:
▪Women’s contributions to development have not been recognized: Role as food producers, carers, educators vs. (male) cash-earning activities
▪Problem identified by WID feminists: “women’s inequality of access to and participation in the definition of economic structures and policies and the productive process itself”
▪Integrationist approach:
▪Women often perform unpaid household/farm labor → incorporate them more into income-earning activities
▪Removing obstacles to women’s advancement in the public sphere, e.g.
Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women
(CEDAW), adopted by UN assembly in 1979
▪Focus on legislative changes, e.g. women’s right to own land
McMichael (2012)

## 第 13 张｜GENDER AND DEVELOPMENT (GAD)

GENDER AND DEVELOPMENT (GAD)
▪GAD feminism argues that remedies need to go even further:
▪Not enough to address who is doing what and whether it is valued → ask
HOW activities come to be valued, by whom, and in whose interests
▪Critique of development discourse:
▪What counts as “productive” work under our economic model?
▪Value is attached to a narrow range of cash-generating activities
▪UN System of National Accounts records only additions to GNP, but not
“costs”: the invisible work of social reproduction, costs to the environment, etc. No debit side.
▪“Agenda-setting” approach:
▪Directly challenge conventional development thinking with a feminist perspective (Jahan 1995)
McMichael (2012)

## 第 14 张｜FROM EQUALITY TO EMPOWERMENT

FROM EQUALITY TO EMPOWERMENT
▪Declaration for Economic Justice and Women’s Empowerment (1999):
▪Redefine economic efficiency to include and value women’s unpaid and paid work
▪Efficiency should be measured by effective realization of human development and human rights, rather than growth, trade, corporate profits
▪Changing gender relations through “empowerment”
▪Rise of transnational feminist networks and movements, e.g. anti-sweatshop movement (Kabeer 2018)
▪Projects designed and run by women from the South
▪Female-only policy interventions
Moser (1993), Willis (2011),
McMichael (2012)

## 第 15 张｜THEORY → PRACTICE (?)

THEORY → PRACTICE (?)
Conditional cash transfer
▪To “empower” women by giving them cash, in exchange for “co-responsibility”:
▪Ensuring their children’s attendance at school and medical check-ups
▪Doing “voluntary” community work, e.g. cleaning schools and health centers
Microfinance
▪To “empower” women by giving them micro-loans: access to credit
Intended achievements
▪Greater female control over household income
▪More direct engagement with public institutions: build relationships between women and the state and public policy
[图]

## 第 16 张｜THEORY → PRACTICE (?)

THEORY → PRACTICE (?)
▪These schemes are supposed to support and empower women, but had complex actual effects (Chant 2015)
▪Intensifying their paid and unpaid work
▪Conditional cash transfer ended up placing even more pressure on women and intensified their unpaid maternal and community role → women end up as “duty-bearers for household poverty alleviation” (Chant 2016)
▪For microfinance, pressure of loan repayment forced women to work even harder as wage laborers at the expense of building up their own enterprises
▪Reinforcing traditional gender norms
▪Women and girls as “chief victims and caretakers” (Resurrection 2011): No attempt to involve men and boys in the process
▪“Feminization of responsibility and/or obligation”: Entrenchment of non-egalitarian model of the family
▪“Women end up working for development, rather than development serving primarily to further women’s interests” (Chant 2016)

## 第 19 张｜Across the world, many government and non-government actors 

Across the world, many government and non-government actors have adopted policies and programmes to remedy gender inequality in their society. Some worked to improve women’s rights and access, others provided financial aid to women, still others focused on organizing women and developing women-run initiatives.
Examine one such experimentation in a society of your choice. How was the policy/programme designed; what was the underlying approach to addressing gender inequality? How did the designer of the policy/programme measure their own effectiveness in addressing gender inequality, and do you agree with their measurement? From your group’s perspective, evaluate the achievements and limitations of the policy/programme. Why did it succeed or fail?
