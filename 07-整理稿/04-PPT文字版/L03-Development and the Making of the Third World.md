## 第 6 张｜THE METROPOLIS-SATELLITE MODEL

THE METROPOLIS-SATELLITE MODEL
▪Frank’s model of how underdevelopment works
▪Originated in the colonial era, when colonial powers implanted new cities in Third World countries to facilitate the transfer of economic surplus to the West
▪These national cities serve as satellites to the
Western metropolis
▪National cities are in turn served by provincial cities, and provincial cities by local cities
▪A whole chain of constellations established to extract raw materials, commodities, profits from
Third World villages to local capitals, to regional capitals, to national capitals, and finally to the cities of Western countries

## 第 24 张｜LOGIC OF ISI

LOGIC OF ISI
▪Instead of importing First World goods, Third World countries to produce those goods themselves
▪Strategy: Restrict foreign imports by measures such as (1) tariffs (2) quotas (3) content regulations
▪These restrictions raised the price of foreign imports
▪Local industries (who normally could not compete with foreign suppliers) now faced a benign market and could begin producing the good locally
▪Governments also supported these infant industries through:
▪Licensing schemes: limit the number of local firms allowed to produce a given product, in effect giving them a legal monopoly
▪Provision of subsidies and cheap credit
(Rapley 2007: 30-33)

## 第 25 张｜POPULARITY OF ISI

POPULARITY OF ISI
▪Turkey and Latin America as earliest adopters in the 1930s
▪Chile as the boldest experimenter: 140 public firms created by the government between 1940 and 1970
▪Import licensing and tariffs (sometimes exceeding 100%) sheltered the local market from foreign competition
▪After WW2, spread to Middle East and South Asia
▪India adopted Soviet-style planning with a focus on heavy industry, together with a tightly-controlled private economy where local firms were favored by protective tariffs and import prohibition
▪As African countries gained independence in the 1960s, many also adopted ISI
(Rapley 2007: 35-41)

## 第 26 张｜ACHIEVEMENTS OF ISI

ACHIEVEMENTS OF ISI
▪In the 1930s, when the world economy was stagnating, Turkey’s annual economic growth rate reached 7%
▪In Latin America, healthy growth rates throughout 1940s and 1950s
▪During India’s first Five-Year Plan (1951-56), national income grew by
18%
(Rapley 2007: 41)

## 第 29 张｜PROBLEMS WITH ISI

PROBLEMS WITH ISI
Inefficiency
▪Lack of competition →firms became inefficient, productivity was low
▪Profits were guaranteed →firms had low incentives to improve
Poor export performance
▪Being protected from competition, the quality of goods produced was simply too low to find market overseas
▪Third World countries were selling more manufactured goods to each other, but not to First World countries →still relied on primary exports to the First World for foreign currency earnings

Corruption and inequality

## 第 41 张｜DEVELOPMENTAL STATE

DEVELOPMENTAL STATE
▪Neoclassical economists: Asian NIEs adopted market-based policies and participated in the world market →success
▪However, contrary to neoclassical theory which advocates a non-interventionist state, Asian NIEs all had governments that played an active role in guiding economic development (to a lesser extent in HK)
▪E.g. South Korean state:
▪Protected infant industries through tariffs and quotas
▪Supported domestic firms through export subsidies and credit
▪Owned and controlled all commercial banks and used them to direct funds toward favored industries
▪Set export targets and rewarded firms that met/exceeded them
▪Limited number of firms allowed to enter an industry
▪Set controls on prices and capital outflows
Rapley (2007:139-144)

## 第 42 张｜DIFFERENCES FROM ISI

DIFFERENCES FROM ISI
▪Rather than building an industrial base to satisfy local demand, EOI focuses on export industries in manufactured goods →larger market
▪Rather than providing protection to all local industries, the government
“chooses winners”
▪Only a few industries were selectively nurtured (e.g. electronics, textile)
▪Within those industries, the government decides which firms they will raise to maturity, and which will be left to die
▪A time limit is placed on protection: factory managers know how long they have to build up their capabilities before they will be thrown onto the world market. Competitive ones survive, inefficient ones allowed to fail.
[图]
